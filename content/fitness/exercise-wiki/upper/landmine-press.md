**Goal:** An overhead-pressing alternative that's easier on the shoulder joint than a straight barbell or dumbbell press, trained from a stance that mimics an athletic ready position.

### Execution
With one end of the barbell anchored in a landmine sleeve (or a corner), stand in a staggered stance — one foot forward, one back, like a athletic setup rather than square feet — and press the free end up and slightly forward. Keep the core braced against the urge to lean or rotate as the bar travels up and away from the body.

### Why it works (theory)
The landmine's arced bar path keeps the shoulder out of the fully overhead, end-range position that straight overhead pressing can aggravate, while the staggered stance adds a stability/anti-rotation demand that a seated or square-stance press doesn't. This is presented as mechanical reasoning, not a cited finding.

### Programming notes
3 x 8-10. Keep the rep controlled on the way down — don't let the bar crash back to the anchor point.
