**Goal:** Hip-to-shoulder force transfer — the same kinetic chain used in a punch, a pitch, or a bat/racquet swing.

### Execution
One end of the barbell anchored in a landmine sleeve. Start with the bar near the hip on one side. Drive from the rear hip and rotate the torso as you press the bar up and across the body, letting the rotation finish the press rather than muscling it up with the arm alone. Reset and repeat for the set on one side before switching.

### Why it works (theory)
Rotational athletic movements (throwing, punching, swinging) generate most of their force from the hips and torso, with the arm largely transmitting rather than generating that force. Training the press to originate from a hip drive rather than a square-stance shoulder push is intended to reinforce that sequencing under load. This is mechanical/sport-specific reasoning, not a cited finding.

### Programming notes
3 x 8 per side. Prioritize the hip-drive-into-rotation sequencing over the weight on the bar — if the rep turns into an arm press, the load is too heavy or the technique has broken down.
