**Goal:** Heavy posterior-chain pulling strength — the low-handle counterpart to the high-handle hexbar deadlift, biased toward the hips and hamstrings instead of the quads.

### Execution
Step inside the bar and grip the low (flat-side) handles. Set the hips back further than you would on the high-handle version, brace the trunk hard, and drive the floor away, keeping the bar path vertical and the spine neutral. Lower under control by pushing the hips back first.

### Why it works (theory)
The lower handle height increases the hip-hinge range of motion, shifting more of the work onto the glutes and hamstrings than the more upright, quad-dominant high-handle pull. The centered hex-bar load still keeps the bar path over the middle of the foot, which is generally thought to reduce shear stress on the lumbar spine compared to a straight-bar deadlift — this hasn't been separately verified for the flat-handle variant specifically, so treat the back-friendliness claim as inherited from the high-handle research rather than confirmed for this exact setup.

### Programming notes
3 x 6-8, heavy. Brace before every rep and reset from the floor between reps rather than bouncing. If the lower back rounds before the target reps are done, the set is over.
