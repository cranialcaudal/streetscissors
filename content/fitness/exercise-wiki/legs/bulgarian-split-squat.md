**Goal:** Single-leg strength and hip stability — the closest gym analog to a pedal stroke, and a left/right imbalance corrector.

### Execution
Elevate the rear foot on a bench behind you. Hold a single kettlebell goblet-style at the chest (or a plate) rather than a dumbbell in each hand. Squat down on the front leg until the rear knee nearly brushes the floor, then drive back up through the front heel. Elevate the front foot slightly on a plate for more quad bias if the goal is climbing/pedaling power specifically.

### Why it works (theory)
Single-leg loaded work asks each leg to produce force and stabilize the hip independently, which a bilateral squat can mask if one side is doing more of the work. The goblet/front-loaded hold demands more anti-flexion core bracing than holding dumbbells at the sides, and the movement pattern — one leg driving through a flexed hip and knee while the other trails behind — resembles the down-stroke of pedaling more closely than a standard squat does. This is a mechanical/logical inference, not a cited claim.

### Programming notes
3 x 8-10 per leg. Keep the front knee tracking over the ankle rather than caving inward, and control the descent rather than dropping into the bottom.
