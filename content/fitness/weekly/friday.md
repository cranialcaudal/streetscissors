**Where:** The pool, on office weeks only — a lunch swim rather than a pre-work one, walkable from the office. Remote the rest of the time, which is most weeks.
**Focus:** Fast-twitch and in-water survivability on office Fridays, on the weeks it happens.

**On office Fridays:** If Monday is the engine, Friday is the gearbox and the airbags. Two hours, three jobs: hold open-water distance, survive without a wall under you, and find a genuine fast gear over short distances. Where Monday stays easy on purpose, Friday deliberately pushes intensity and skill. The swim carves out a two-hour lunch block from the workday — this is the *harder* of your two swim days.

The session is built to land on the upper body and core — shoulders, lats, treading legs, breath control — while sparing the running muscles you'll need for Saturday. That's why the swimrun run segments stay short and smooth: they're transitions, not a workout. Get the speed and the survival work in, keep the legs fresh, and you arrive at Saturday ready.

**On remote Fridays:** No pool access that week, so the hard swim simply doesn't happen — and that's fine. Instead, run a Fartlek session: unstructured speed play, alternating hard surges and easy jogging by feel rather than a fixed interval script. It's a genuine change of stimulus from the swim, keeps some intensity in the week, and still leaves Saturday's legs alone if you keep the surges controlled rather than all-out.

> Five days clear of Sunday's pitch and two days ahead of the next one — this is the right window to ask the shoulder for speed, on the weeks you get the pool. If anything sharp shows up, drop the fast block and keep the endurance and treading. On Fartlek weeks the shoulder isn't in play at all, so run it as hard as the legs allow.
